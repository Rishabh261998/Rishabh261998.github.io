About

Engineer by trade, researcher by habit.

I'm a Founding AI Engineer at Cekura, where we build the testing and observability platform for conversational AI. My corner of the product is everything needed to run a simulation and judge it: generating realistic scenarios and personas from an agent's prompt and tools, running thousands of simulated calls with LLM-driven testers, scoring each conversation with evaluation metrics, and optimizing those metrics against customer feedback. Along the way I've shipped an end-of-call algorithm that cut bad hang-ups to under 1% in production and red-teaming flows that probe what an agent should never say.

Before Cekura I finished my MS in Computer Science at Purdue University (2023–2025, GPA 3.9), where I took the deep end of the ML catalogue: NLP, deep learning, large language models, reasoning in LLMs and LLM alignment. There I worked with Prof. Abulhair Saparov on how much factual information a transformer can actually store, work that is currently under review at ICLR.

I graduated from IIT Kanpur in 2021 with a BS in Mathematics and Scientific Computing and a minor in Machine Learning. Under Prof. Ashutosh Modi and Prof. Arnab Bhattacharya I co-authored two Legal AI papers (ACL 2021 and EMNLP 2022) on predicting and explaining court judgments and on segmenting legal documents. I then spent a year as a software engineer at Oracle, contributed Bayesian visualization plots to ArviZ through Google Summer of Code, and interned at Goldman Sachs.

Deep nets still drive me a little crazy, in the good way. I care about systems that are measurably correct, which is why I keep ending up at the intersection of LLM engineering and evaluation. Outside work I solve puzzles, binge anime, play multiplayer games, and pick up a bat or racquet for cricket, table tennis and badminton. I also used to grind competitive programming and hold a 1800+ rating on Codeforces.
